On Monday, Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5.2, one of the strongest models you can access today.

Buried deep in the launch announcement is the fact that, during training, they deliberately held the model back in two areas, among others: optimising code for AI chips and improving the architecture of language models. In those areas, it performs at about the level of the older Opus 5.5.

That is exactly what you need to build or run your own AI.

At home, I run open models on my own graphics cards. Whenever I want to get more out of them, I end up in GPU code and in how the model is built. The tool I use most is now deliberately weaker in precisely those areas.

The reasoning makes sense. AI has begun to speed up the development of AI. The evaluation group METR estimates that Anthropic is now improving its models about twice as fast as before, probably mostly because they use AI in the work. They do not want to give everyone access to that loop. In June, they did this secretly and had to apologise. Now it is out in the open.

The brake applies only to us.

Anthropic has an internal model that is better than the one we get. This week, they told investors that their own model moved Claude onto AMD chips in three days and wrote almost all the GPU code itself — a job that, according to them, used to take six months to a year.

So we have two floors. Upstairs are those who get to build AI at full strength. Downstairs are those of us who get to use it.

Ten days ago, I wrote about second-class citizens with second-class AI. That was about filters that stop you. This time, nothing stops you. The model does the job, just less well in precisely this area, and that is baked into the training itself.

For Europe, this is AI sovereignty in practice. If we are to have our own models, or even just run open models efficiently on our own hardware, we need people and tools that can do exactly this. One of the best tools on the market has been held back precisely there.

I do not doubt that the safety concern is real. The result, nevertheless, is that those in the lead keep the tool that keeps them in the lead.

Who in Norway is building and training their own models today, and what do you use when the Americans hold back?
