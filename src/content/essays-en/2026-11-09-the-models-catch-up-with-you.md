Over the past year, I have built an agent setup at home. The agents work while I sleep, remember what we have done and carry the tasks forward themselves.

This autumn, the big players arrived with the same thing. In September, OpenAI launched “dots”: always-on agents with their own computers in the cloud, access to your apps and memory that carries the work forward between conversations. From 18 November, they will be standard in ChatGPT Enterprise unless an administrator says no. Three weeks later, Anthropic introduced Claude Duet. It sends you a morning briefing, keeps an eye on your apps and continues working when you put down your phone.

What I have spent a year building is becoming a standard feature.

There is no technical moat in AI. If you are ahead, you might be six months ahead. Who remembers prompt engineering? What took me days to accomplish six months ago, agents now do in minutes.

Many think they are digging a moat. I think most of us are pushing a bow wave ahead of us. It exists only as long as you keep up your speed. Stop pushing, and the sea swallows it. And it takes effort every single second. All the scaffolding we build around the models' weaknesses is drag on the wave.

My setup at home is still useful. But the value has always been in what I use it for.

What lasts longest is understanding the problem: knowing what is worth building, for whom and why. Two weeks ago, I wrote that even that has an expiry date. It still lasts longer than any trick.

So put your energy into the cargo, not the wave.
